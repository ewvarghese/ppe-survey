Field checklist — AI PPE detection site survey

Print this or keep it open on your phone. Tick items in the web form as you go.
Open form

ACarry on site

  • Phone with camera + this checklist; power bank
  • Torch (for dark bays), laser/tape measure (mounting heights & distances)
  • Laptop with VLC (to open an RTSP test stream) and a PoE/cable tester if available
  • PPE for yourself: helmet, shoes, vest, goggles — you will be asked to wear them
  • Blank visitor/NDA forms; company profile one-pager to leave behind

1Open the meeting right

  • Confirm who decides: safety head vs plant head vs IT vs purchase — get the real decision maker's name even if not in the room.
  • Ask their pain first, say nothing about price: "Walk me through your last PPE-related incident or notice."
  • Count people: employees, contract workers, max per shift.

2State our accuracy honestly — early

Say this: "Our model detects PPE violations at about 85% accuracy in testing. On your site the number depends on your cameras — which is what we're measuring today — and we improve it by training on your own footage during a pilot."
  • Then ask: "What accuracy would you need to act on an alert?" → record their minimum (section 08).
  • Ask about false alarms separately: "How many wrong alerts per shift would make your team ignore the system?"
  • Never promise 95%+ on day one. Promise a pilot with a written success criterion instead.

3Walk every zone you will monitor

  • For each zone: photograph the live camera view (screen photo of the monitor is fine) + one wide shot of the area.
  • Note: mounting height, distance to workers, tilt angle, lighting at that hour, glare, dust/steam.
  • Check occlusion: can you see a worker's full body, or only head/shoulders behind machines? Head-only frames kill shoe & vest detection.
  • Check night-shift lighting in the same spot if the visit is in daytime — ask for a night photo from the NVR.
  • Mark blind spots where a new camera would be needed; estimate count (section 11).

4CCTV room / NVR — 10 minutes that decide the project

  • Photograph the NVR/DVR label: make, model, channel count.
  • Open the camera list: note IP vs analogue per camera, resolution & FPS as configured.
  • Open one live view at full screen — judge sharpness yourself (section 04 rating).
  • Ask for RTSP/ONVIF access and who in IT can grant it.
  • Retention days and free disk space.
Analogue/DVR site? Don't reject it: an encoder or camera replacement is the standard fix — capture it in section 04 and price it later.

5Server room — photograph, don't guess

  • Photos: rack front, free U-space, UPS, AC unit, the server's back panel.
  • Server label: make/model, CPU, RAM; open OS and read version if allowed.
  • GPU: run nvidia-smi if a GPU exists — screenshot it. No GPU is fine, it just means new hardware.
  • Power point + cooling near the rack; free PCIe slot if a GPU card is the plan.

6The money questions (section 07 & 10)

  • "If we prove this works on your cameras, how would you prefer to buy it — one-time purchase, or monthly?"
  • "Would a new server be part of your budget, or should we fit inside what you already have?" → captures willingness to invest without quoting a price.
  • Ask the budget band after they answer, not before.
  • Approval route & timeline; tender or direct purchase; who else is quoting.
  • ROI in their words: cost of one accident, penalty history, audit pressure, customer demands.

7Close with a dated next step

  • Agree one next step with a date: demo / footage test / technical audit / quotation.
  • Ask for recorded footage (even 2 hours from 2 cameras) — it is your fastest accuracy proof.
  • Get consent: site photos, technical audit visit, footage test.
  • Before leaving the gate: submit the form, or at least download the JSON copy.

8Score it honestly (section 11)

  • Video quality: can YOU see helmet + shoes + vest clearly in the live view? If not, score ≤ 5 and recommend camera fixes.
  • Server readiness: no rack/power/cooling = low score, edge box may be the answer.
  • Buying intent 0–10: would they sign this month? If not, say why in obstacles.
